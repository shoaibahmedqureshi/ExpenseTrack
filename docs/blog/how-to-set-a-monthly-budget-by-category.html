How to set a monthly budget by category and stick to it

17 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · All articles

A budget is a plan for your money, and a plan only helps if it is based on how you really live. Here is a simple way to build a monthly budget by category that you can actually follow.

Start with what you really spend

Look at the last two or three months of spending before you set a single limit. Budgets built from wishful thinking fail in the first week. Add up each category and take the average. That average is your honest starting point.

Separate fixed costs from flexible ones

Rent, loan payments, insurance and subscriptions are fixed: you know the amount in advance. Food, transport, shopping and fun are flexible. Put your fixed costs in first, then divide what remains between the flexible categories. Most of your saving will come from the flexible ones.

A rule of thumb to begin with

A popular starting point is to aim for roughly half of your income on needs, thirty percent on wants and twenty percent on savings or debt repayment. It is only a guide. If your rent is high, your numbers will look different, and that is fine. Adjust the split to your situation and keep what works.

Add a buffer

Every month has something unplanned: a repair, a gift, a medical bill. Set aside a small buffer category so one surprise does not break the whole plan. If you do not use it, move it to savings.

Check in mid-month

A budget you only read at the end of the month is a report, not a tool. Look at where each category stands around the middle of the month. If one is already close to its limit, you still have two weeks to adjust.

Let the app show what is left

In Outlay you set monthly limits by category and see exactly how much you have left, in real time, as you log expenses. At the end of the month, the reports show where you stayed on track and where you did not, so next month's limits can be better.

Your first budget will not be perfect. Treat it as a draft, adjust it after a month, and it will get closer to your real life every time.
