How to track your expenses in five minutes a week

17 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · All articles

Most people do not give up on tracking their spending because they stop caring. They give up because it feels like a second job. The fix is not more discipline, it is a smaller routine. This is a five-minute-a-week habit that works for most people.

1. Record it when it happens

The longer you wait, the more you forget. Log an expense in the moment: when you pay, while you wait for the bill, or on the way out. A few seconds now saves a long, fuzzy reconstruction at the end of the month. If you do not have signal, log it anyway and let it sync later.

2. Use fewer categories than you think you need

Twenty categories look organised but make every entry a decision. Start with eight to ten: housing, food, transport, bills, health, shopping, fun, and one catch-all. You can always split a category later, once you see where the money really goes.

3. Do a five-minute weekly check

Pick one day, for example Sunday evening, and spend five minutes on three questions:

  • Is anything missing, such as cash spending or a bill I forgot to log?
  • Is anything in the wrong category?
  • Which category is running ahead of where I expected?

That last question is the useful one. A small overspend caught on day eight is easy to correct. The same overspend discovered on day thirty is a surprise.

4. Look at the month once, not daily

Checking totals every day makes tracking stressful, and stress is what makes people quit. Look at the monthly picture once, compare it with last month, and change one thing. One change at a time is how habits stick.
