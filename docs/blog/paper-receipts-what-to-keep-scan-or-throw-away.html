Paper receipts: what to keep, what to scan and what to throw away

17 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · All articles

Receipts are easy to collect and hard to find when you need them. Here is a practical way to decide what to keep, what to scan and what to throw away.

Keep these

  • Anything with a warranty, such as phones, laptops and appliances. The receipt is your proof of purchase date.
  • Big purchases you might want to return, insure or resell.
  • Expenses you may need to claim, for example work costs or anything connected to your taxes.

How long you must keep financial records depends on where you live and your situation, so check the rules that apply to you or ask an accountant.

Scan these early

Many shop receipts are printed on thermal paper, and the print can fade over time. A receipt that is clear today may be blank in a year. Photograph or scan the ones that matter soon after you get them, not at the end of the year.

Throw these away

Small everyday receipts you will never return, claim or need for a warranty can go once you have logged the expense. Do not keep a shoebox of paper just in case.

Make receipts easy to find later

  • Capture the merchant, date, total and tax. These are what you will search for.
  • Store the photo with the expense entry, not in a separate folder you will forget about.
  • Use the same category names every time so reports are consistent.
